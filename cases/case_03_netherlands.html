1. Abstract

The Netherlands deployed three parallel contact tracing mechanisms during the COVID-19 pandemic: informal tracing, manual contact tracing by regional public health services, and digital contact tracing via the CoronaMelder Bluetooth app launched on 10 October 2020.[5] The three systems operated in parallel without data sharing.

Informal tracing was triggered by the case themselves, without official involvement: once a case received a positive test result, they could directly notify people in their social network. Notified contacts had no legal obligation to isolate and acted voluntarily. Manual tracing involved GGD staff interviewing cases by phone and issuing legal quarantine orders to identified contacts. App notifications were advisory only, with no legal enforcement.

A modelling study estimated that informal tracing contributed a 6.0% reduction in R, nearly equivalent to the 6.4% contributed by the entire national manual tracing system.[2] An empirical study found that among asymptomatic contacts, 57.8%–64.8% reported being warned directly by the index case, far exceeding digital and manual contact tracing notifications.[1] Household notification had the shortest exposure-to-test interval, with a mean of 3.1 days.[1]

Key limitation: informal notification is not systematically recorded in any surveillance system, and its contribution to epidemic control can only be inferred through modelling or behavioural surveys.

2. Background

2.1 Epidemic context

The Netherlands confirmed its first COVID-19 case on 27 February 2020, a 56-year-old man who had returned from northern Italy.[3] The epidemic progressed through four tracing-relevant phases.

PhasePeriodDominant variantContext relevant to contact tracing
1Feb–Jun 2020Wild-typeSmall-scale GGD manual tracing; limited testing capacity; no digital app.
2Oct 2020–May 2021Wild-type / AlphaCoronaMelder app launched October 2020; study period for both key papers.
3May–Dec 2021DeltaMCT regularly overwhelmed; cases asked to self-notify contacts; vaccination rollout.
4Dec 2021–Mar 2022OmicronMCT de facto suspended; from February 2022, cases with positive self-test no longer required PCR confirmation.

Total pandemic burden was 8,657,484 confirmed cases and 22,986 deaths in a population of approximately 17.5 million, corresponding to a CFR of 0.27%.[3]

2.2 Enabling conditions

Two structural factors are directly relevant. First, the Netherlands operates a decentralised public health system through 25 regional GGD agencies. GGD capacity for manual contact tracing was sized for small outbreaks, not pandemic-scale transmission. During high-case periods, cases were explicitly asked by GGD to notify their own contacts, blurring the boundary between formal manual tracing and informal tracing in the data.

Second, the Netherlands chose the decentralised Google/Apple Exposure Notification framework for CoronaMelder, prioritising privacy over surveillance capacity. The app could not share data with GGD tracers in real time, and until November 2021 cases needed a GGD-issued code before they could upload their positive status, introducing a structural delay in the digital pathway.

3. Contact Tracing Strategy

3.1 System architecture

The Netherlands operated three parallel and structurally independent contact notification mechanisms during the study period from December 2020 to May 2021. None of the three pathways shared data. A single positive case could simultaneously trigger all three.

Informal pathway

  1. Case tests positive, by any test type.
  2. Case directly contacts known people by phone, text, or in person.
  3. Contact voluntarily self-isolates, with no legal obligation.
  4. Contact books test voluntarily.
  5. Contact pool: known contacts only.

Manual pathway: GGD

  1. Case tests positive; result enters the GGD / HPZone system.
  2. GGD tracer interviews the case.
  3. Case recalls recent contacts by name and phone number.
  4. GGD contacts each identified person individually by phone.
  5. Contact receives a legal 10-day quarantine obligation.

Digital pathway: CoronaMelder

  1. Case tests positive and manually uploads key to the app.
  2. App detects Bluetooth contacts meeting threshold.
  3. App anonymously notifies qualifying contacts around 15 minutes after upload.
  4. Contact receives advisory recommendation only.
  5. Contact pool includes anonymous Bluetooth-detected contacts.

3.3 Implementation phases

Phase 1 — Manual only (February 2020–9 October 2020)

GGD manual tracing operated from the outset, but at limited scale. Informal self-notification by cases was the dominant mechanism by which contacts learned of exposure, but it was not systematically measured or recorded by any surveillance system.

Phase 2 — Manual + digital hybrid (10 October 2020–around February 2022)

CoronaMelder launched on 10 October 2020.[5] By the end of May 2021, almost 30% of the Dutch population had downloaded the app, but only around 18% were still actively using it.[1] During high-case periods, MCT capacity was regularly exceeded and GGD asked cases to notify their own close contacts directly.

Phase 3 — De facto informal only (from around February 2022)

During the Omicron wave, MCT was effectively suspended at population level due to system overload. Cases with positive self-tests were no longer required to confirm with a PCR test or enter the GGD tracing programme. Informal self-notification became the only functioning contact tracing mechanism for the general population during this period.

4. Notification & Quarantine Mechanism

4.1 Cases — PCR-positive or rapid-test-positive individuals

Trigger
Positive PCR test or positive rapid antigen test from March 2021 onwards. Cases with positive self-tests were initially required to confirm with PCR; from February 2022, a self-test positive was sufficient trigger.
Isolation location
Home only. No central quarantine facility option.
Duration
10 days from symptom onset or positive test date if asymptomatic.
Legal status
Legally enforced during most of the pandemic. Breach was a criminal offence.

4.2 Contacts — Informal notification

Trigger
Exposure-triggered. The index case directly contacts known people in their social network, typically by phone, text message, or in person.
Who is notified
Known contacts only: family, friends, colleagues, and housemates. Anonymous or forgotten contacts are not covered.
Isolation
Voluntary self-isolation at home. No legally enforceable quarantine obligation arises from informal notification alone.
Legal status
None. Entirely voluntary. Contact behaviour depends on social norms, trust in the case, and individual risk perception.

4.3 Contacts — Manual tracing notification, GGD/MCT

Trigger
Exposure-triggered. GGD notifies contacts identified during the case interview, when MCT capacity is available.
Isolation location
Home quarantine only.
Duration
10 days from last exposure.
Legal status
Legally enforced. GGD-issued quarantine carries legal obligation. When MCT capacity was exceeded and cases were asked to self-notify, the resulting self-isolation had no legal basis.

4.4 Contacts — Digital app notification, CoronaMelder/DCT

Trigger
Exposure-triggered. Case uploads positive test key to CoronaMelder backend; a GGD-issued code was required until November 2021, with self-upload enabled thereafter. The app auto-notified qualifying Bluetooth contacts approximately 15 minutes after upload.
Isolation
Advisory self-isolation recommended.
Legal status
Advisory only — no legal obligation. This is structurally equivalent to England's NHS COVID-19 app.

Unlike manual and digital tracing, informal tracing has no administrative data. Coverage estimates come from behavioural survey data and modelling that back-calculates the informal tracing contribution from epidemic data.

The high positivity among household-notified contacts (22.8%) reflects the accuracy advantage of informal tracing: cases notify people with whom they had genuine high-risk contact, producing a high signal-to-noise ratio compared with Bluetooth-based digital tracing (3.4%–6.1%).[1]

5.4 Rt / epidemic-level impact

Not available as a clean independent estimate. The modelling estimate of 6.0% R reduction for informal tracing is the closest available figure, but it is a simulation output for December 2020–March 2021 and should not be generalised to other epidemic phases or variants.[2]

6. Limitations & Failure Points

  • No systematic data collection. Informal tracing is invisible to surveillance systems and appears in data only as the absence of DCT or MCT notification, inferred from behavioural survey responses.[1]
  • Voluntary compliance. Informally notified contacts have no legal obligation to isolate. Compliance depends on social norms, relationship to the case, and individual risk perception.
  • Coverage ceiling: known contacts only. Informal tracing cannot reach anonymous contacts such as strangers, commuters, or co-diners. During periods when superspreader events drove transmission, informal tracing would have been structurally unable to break these chains.[2]
  • System-overflow confounding. During MCT-overwhelmed periods, GGD instructed cases to self-notify contacts. In empirical data, these system-directed self-notifications are indistinguishable from purely voluntary informal tracing.
  • Recall bias in survey data. Behavioural surveys on reasons for testing rely on self-report and may suffer misclassification, especially where contacts received both formal and informal notifications.

7. Model Implications

Note: preliminary — to be confirmed with Joshua.
  1. Informal tracing should be modelled as a third separate compartment. The Netherlands data demonstrate that informal self-notification is not a negligible background effect but a mechanism contributing as much to R reduction as the national manual tracing system (6.0% vs 6.4%).[2]
  2. Informal tracing ρ can be parameterised from survey data. The 57.8%–64.8% index notification rate among asymptomatic contacts provides an empirical upper bound for informal tracing coverage in a high-trust, high-compliance European setting.[1]
  3. Informal tracing has the fastest notification speed. Household notification achieved an exposure-to-test interval of 3.1 days, equal to symptom-triggered testing and faster than DCT and MCT.[1]
  4. Informal tracing has the highest precision. Household-notified contacts had a 22.8% positivity rate, compared with 17.6% for MCT and 3.4%–6.1% for DCT.[1]
  5. Legal enforcement is absent in informal tracing. Compliance is entirely driven by social norms. The model should apply a compliance modifier lower than MCT, but potentially higher than anonymous advisory DCT because of the personal relationship between case and contact.
  6. Overflow dynamics matter. When MCT capacity is exceeded, informal tracing becomes the de facto primary mechanism. Models should include a threshold at which official systems saturate and informal tracing absorbs the residual tracing burden.
